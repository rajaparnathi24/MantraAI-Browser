ऋग्वेद · मण्डल १० · सूक्त १२५

देवी सूक्तम्

स्रोत: ऋग्वेद, मण्डल १०, सूक्त १२५ · ऋषिका: वाक् आम्भृणी

विषय: वाक् (दिव्य वाणी) की शक्ति और व्यापकता

सम्पूर्ण संस्कृत पाठ

॥ ॐ ॥

  1. १

    अहं रुद्रेभिर्वसुभिश्चराम्यहमादित्यैरुत विश्वदेवैः। अहं मित्रावरुणोभा बिभर्म्यहमिन्द्राग्नी अहमश्विनोभा॥

  2. २

    अहं सोममाहनसं बिभर्म्यहं त्वष्टारमुत पूषणं भगम्। अहं दधामि द्रविणं हविष्मते सुप्राव्ये यजमानाय सुन्वते॥

  3. ३

    अहं राष्ट्री संगमनी वसूनां चिकितुषी प्रथमा यज्ञियानाम्। तां मा देवा व्यदधुः पुरुत्रा भूरिस्थात्रां भूर्यावेशयन्तीम्॥

  4. ४

    मया सोऽन्नमत्ति यो विपश्यति यः प्राणिति य ईं शृणोत्युक्तम्। अमन्तवो मां त उप क्षियन्ति श्रुधि श्रुत श्रद्धिवं ते वदामि॥

  5. ५

    अहमेव स्वयंमिदं वदामि जुष्टं देवेभिरुत मानुषेभिः। यं कामये तं तमुग्रं कृणोमि तं ब्रह्माणं तमृषिं तं सुमेधाम्॥

  6. ६

    अहं रुद्राय धनुरातनोमि ब्रह्मद्विषे शरवे हन्तवा उ। अहं जनाय समदं कृणोम्यहं द्यावापृथिवी आविवेश॥

  7. ७

    अहं सुवे पितरमस्य मूर्धन्मम योनिरप्स्वन्तः समुद्रे। ततो वितिष्ठे भुवनानु विश्वोतामूं द्यां वर्ष्मणोपस्पृशामि॥

  8. ८

    अहमेव वात इव प्रवाम्यारभमाणा भुवनानि विश्वा। परो दिवा पर एना पृथिव्या एतावती महिना सम्बभूव॥

॥ ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः ॥

Meaning and context

Source: Rigveda, Mandala 10, Sukta 125 — Devi Sukta (Vāk Sukta). Seer: Vāk Ambhṛṇī. Its central theme is the divine power of speech, consciousness, knowledge, and the force that sustains the universe.

The Devi Sukta presents Vāk, the divine power of speech, as a cosmic force that operates throughout existence. Its deeper message is that speech is not merely a collection of sounds; it is a means through which knowledge is expressed, thoughts are communicated, rituals are performed, and human understanding develops. In the first two verses, Vāk declares her presence among the gods and her role in sustaining divine and sacrificial activities. This presents speech as something fundamental to the religious understanding of the universe.

In the third verse, she describes herself as the sovereign power that brings resources together and supports the order of society. The fourth verse gives a particularly profound idea: human beings eat, breathe, see, and hear through the conditions that sustain life, yet they may fail to recognize the deeper power behind their existence and communication. The fifth verse connects divine grace with the emergence of wisdom, poetic insight, and spiritual understanding. The sixth verse describes a protective and forceful aspect of this power, while the final two verses portray it as a presence that extends throughout the universe and transcends the boundaries of heaven and earth.

How does this relate to the power of mantras?

Within the Hindu spiritual tradition, a mantra is not understood merely as an ordinary sentence. It may combine sacred sound, a particular meaning, disciplined repetition, focused attention, and devotion. Its traditional significance comes from the belief that sound and consciousness are deeply connected. When someone chants a mantra with concentration, the repetition can help direct attention, regulate breathing, reduce mental distraction, and establish a steady meditative practice. These are plausible psychological and physiological effects, although they vary between individuals and mantras. In Vedic and later Hindu traditions, particular mantras also have ritual significance based on their deity, wording, pronunciation, and method of practice.

The Devi Sukta, however, does not explicitly claim that every mantra possesses unlimited supernatural power. Rather, it offers a philosophical foundation for understanding why sacred speech is regarded with reverence. Its vision of Vāk suggests that speech is connected to knowledge, divine order, and the expression of reality itself. The idea that a mantra can transform a person's inner life through disciplined practice is compatible with this interpretation. Claims that a mantra can grant supernatural abilities, control external events, or guarantee miraculous outcomes require separate evidence and should not be treated as established facts.

The deepest takeaway: The power of a mantra, in the traditional Hindu understanding, lies not simply in repeating sounds but in the sacred meaning attributed to them, the discipline of practice, the attention brought to each repetition, and the spiritual understanding cultivated through that practice. The Devi Sukta invites us to look beyond the sound of words and reflect on the profound relationship between speech, consciousness, knowledge, and existence.
